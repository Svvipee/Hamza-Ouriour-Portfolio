Objective & process context

A plasma-assisted fluidized bed reactor (PFBR) fluidizes fine metal-oxide particles and exposes them to reactive gas and plasma. The goal is to improve hydrogen-based metal-oxide reduction by increasing gas–solid contact and reaction rates.

The engineering challenge is that several systems have to work together at once: gas flow that keeps fine particles suspended, plasma that stays stable, cooling that protects the hardware, and particle handling that recovers material without losses.

My responsibility

  • Developing and iterating the PFBR design across multiple versions.
  • Reworking the reactor into a more compact, integrated system with simpler external tubing and flow paths, while keeping gas flow, cooling, plasma, and particle-handling functions intact.
  • Developing water-cooled torch components and improved instrumentation.
  • Characterizing two PFBR prototypes and documenting the operating architecture.

Result

Iterative redesign and system integration improved overall reactor operating efficiency by approximately 35%. The two characterized prototypes and the documented operating architecture set the baseline for future Ar–H₂ testing.

Engineering takeaway: a reactor is only as repeatable as its documentation. Writing down the gas, cooling, and P&ID architecture turned trial runs into a test plan.
