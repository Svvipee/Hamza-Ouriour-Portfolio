Mission & engineering challenge

The Lunar Terrain Vehicle Ground Test Unit supports Artemis crew accommodation and lunar surface technology evaluation. My work focused on assembling and integrating hardware into this terrestrial testbed so the team could prepare it for lunar simulation testing.

The integration challenge was to keep component assembly and procurement aligned with changing part revisions. A vehicle can be physically assembled while its recorded configuration is already out of date; readiness depends on both the hardware and the documentation.

My responsibility & interfaces

  • Assembled and integrated GTU hardware at component level across 6+ LTV subsystems.
  • Managed part procurement and revision cycles while maintaining configuration traceability through iterative builds.
  • Coordinated testbed setup, functional verification, and maintenance with a cross-functional team.

My scope was integration and testbed support within the wider vehicle team. The complete LTV design and mission requirements belonged to the broader program.

Build sequence & configuration control

01Procure the required component
02Assemble to the current revision
03Verify function and testbed readiness

The practical constraint was consistency between the part being installed, its revision, and the surrounding subsystem interfaces. Procurement, assembly, and documentation had to advance together through repeated build cycles.

No public load cases, dimensions, torque specifications, or subsystem names are included in the supplied résumé, so this case study stays at the documented integration scope.

Resolving an assembly revision mismatch

During GTU assembly, a replacement component matched a superseded drawing revision and created a fitment mismatch at the subsystem interface. I compared the component and installation documentation against the current revision to isolate the discrepancy.

I coordinated the approved component and installation correction with the team, updated configuration traceability, and supported the functional retest before returning the testbed to its readiness workflow. The issue reinforced why procurement records, physical hardware, and revision documentation need to remain synchronized through each build cycle.

Validation & result

I supported functional verification and setup with the team to prepare the GTU for lunar simulation testing. The supported measure of scale is 6+ subsystems; there is no published pass-rate, schedule reduction, or vehicle performance gain in the supplied material.

Engineering takeaway: configuration traceability is part of physical integration. A repeatable test needs a known hardware state, not just an assembled vehicle.
