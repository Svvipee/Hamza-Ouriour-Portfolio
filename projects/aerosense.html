Objective & system scope

AeroSense PHM is a Prognostics & Health Management platform focused on BLDC/ball-screw electro-mechanical actuators for commercial aircraft. I built the pipeline from physics simulation through model evaluation and the maintenance dashboard.

The engineering problem is to turn actuator behavior into useful fault and remaining-life information. Classifying a fault and estimating remaining useful life (RUL) are different outputs, so each needs its own validation measure.

My responsibility & implementation

I engineered the end-to-end machine-learning pipeline using Random Forest and Gradient Boosting across 17 physics-grounded features, and benchmarked 5 models. I also built the 7-layer system architecture spanning simulation, feature extraction, API, persistence, and dashboard functionality.

Engineering analysis & tradeoffs

Overall accuracy summarizes classification across the evaluation set. Critical-fault recall specifically measures how many critical cases are detected: recall = TP / (TP + FN). RUL error is a regression measure: RMSE = √[Σ(predicted − reference)² / N], expressed here in cycles.

The reported 64.2-cycle RUL RMSE cannot be interpreted as a percentage or a maintenance interval without the lifetime distribution and evaluation definition. Likewise, high aggregate accuracy alone cannot establish low missed-critical-fault risk.

Engineering concern: model score versus maintenance usefulness

The available record contains benchmark results and architecture, but no documented failed run or model-debugging sequence. The concrete concern is interpretation: classification accuracy, critical-fault recall, and lifetime prediction error answer different maintenance questions.

Reporting all three measures makes that distinction visible. Dataset provenance, train/test separation, degradation conditions, and critical-fault class balance are not supplied, so no fleet deployment, certification, or in-service reliability result is claimed.
